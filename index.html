Before you go

Best time calendar →
When is the best time to visit Northern Pakistan?

May to October for most places. June to September is the only window for high passes and lakes such as Babusar Top, Deosai, Fairy Meadows and the jeep lakes above Kalam. Hunza and Skardu are lovely in April for blossom and in late October for autumn colours. Murree, Nathia Gali and Malam Jabba get snow from December to February.

How much does a trip to the north cost?

A budget road trip costs roughly Rs 15,000 to 25,000 per person for a long weekend in Swat, Naran or Neelum, and Rs 60,000 or more for a week in Hunza and Skardu. Use the trip cost planner for an estimate from Islamabad, Peshawar or Lahore for your group size and hotel level.

Is it safe to travel to Gilgit-Baltistan and Swat?

The main tourist routes see large numbers of domestic and foreign visitors every summer. The biggest real risks are road conditions, landslides after rain and altitude. Check road status before you set out, carry your CNIC or passport for checkposts, and follow local advice near the Line of Control.

Do I need a 4x4 to visit the north?

No for the main towns: an ordinary car reaches Mingora, Kalam, Naran, Gilgit, Hunza, Skardu, Chitral and Neelum. Local jeeps are needed for places like Fairy Meadows, Deosai, Mahodand, Kumrat and Ratti Gali, and you can hire them on the spot.

Which mobile network works best in the mountains?

SCOM and Zong usually have the widest coverage in Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir; Jazz and Telenor work in most towns. Expect no signal in high valleys, so save pages offline before you go.

Can I use KohNoor without internet?

Yes. Install it from your browser menu and open your destinations once while you have signal. Pages you opened, your packing list and the emergency numbers then keep working offline.
